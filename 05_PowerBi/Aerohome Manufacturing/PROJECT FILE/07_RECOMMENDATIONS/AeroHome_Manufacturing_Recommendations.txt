AEROHOME MANUFACTURING — BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS

Purpose

This document translates the AeroHome Manufacturing Power BI dashboard
findings into practical actions for production, quality, maintenance,
energy, finance, procurement, supply chain, and workforce management.

IMPORTANT

Recommendations are based on the dashboard examples discussed in this
project. Confirm current values, filter context, and underlying data
before treating any observation as a verified business issue.

1.  PRODUCTION PLANNING AND PLANT PERFORMANCE

Recommendations: - Compare planned units and produced units by plant,
product category, month, and shift. - Create a production shortfall
measure and rank plants with the largest verified gap. - Review the
schedule, machine availability, staffing, and material availability
during low-achievement periods. - Set realistic monthly production
targets based on demand, capacity, and historical performance. - Track
corrective actions and review whether production achievement improves in
the following period.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Planned Units - Total Produced Units -
Production Achievement % - Production Variance - Production Variance %

2.  QUALITY AND DEFECT REDUCTION

Recommendations: - Prioritize products with both high defect rates and
high estimated defect-cost exposure. - Use Pareto analysis to identify
the small number of products, defect types, or processes contributing
most defects. - Investigate root causes such as material quality,
machine settings, work instructions, and inspection consistency. - Track
corrective and preventive actions with an owner and target completion
date. - Recalculate defect rate as total defective units divided by
total produced units; avoid averaging row-level percentages unless that
is explicitly the intended metric.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Defective Units - Defect Rate % - Estimated
Defect Cost Exposure - Defects by Product / Plant / Machine - Rework or
Scrap Cost, if available

3.  MACHINE MAINTENANCE AND DOWNTIME

Recommendations: - Rank machines and downtime reasons by total downtime
minutes and production impact. - Investigate recurring breakdowns and
schedule preventive maintenance based on equipment criticality and
history. - Record downtime start, end, reason, machine, plant, and shift
consistently. - Compare downtime before and after maintenance actions to
determine whether they worked. - Check operator and machine
relationships if every category displays the same downtime total.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Downtime Minutes - Total Downtime Hours -
Downtime by Machine - Downtime by Reason - Mean Time Between Failures
(MTBF) and Mean Time to Repair (MTTR), if timestamps are available

4.  ENERGY EFFICIENCY

Recommendations: - Monitor energy per unit, calculated as total energy
consumed divided by total produced units. - Compare plants producing
similar product mixes before ranking their energy performance. -
Investigate periods with high energy use but low production output. -
Review idle-running equipment, compressed-air leaks, operating
schedules, and maintenance conditions where relevant. - Set an
energy-reduction target only after establishing a validated baseline.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Energy Consumed (kWh) - Energy per Unit (kWh) -
Energy by Plant / Product / Shift - Energy Cost, if tariff data is
available

5.  PROCUREMENT AND SUPPLIER RELIABILITY

Recommendations: - Review suppliers with repeated late deliveries, long
actual lead times, or low ratings. - Compare expected and actual lead
times using consistent units and date definitions. - Segment supplier
performance by material type and purchase-order value. - Establish
supplier review meetings and corrective-action plans for recurring
delivery issues. - Consider alternative qualified suppliers for critical
materials when the risk assessment supports it.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Purchase Orders - Procurement Spend - Delayed
Orders - Delay Rate % - Expected vs Actual Lead Days - Supplier Rating -
Supplier On-Time Delivery %

6.  FINANCE AND COST CONTROL

Recommendations: - Review high-spend suppliers and materials to identify
negotiation or sourcing opportunities. - Compare unit cost with target
price by product and investigate large variances. - Separate actual
costs from estimated cost exposure and potential production value. -
Validate currency, cost definitions, and units before combining
measures. - Prioritize improvement projects using estimated savings,
implementation cost, operational risk, and payback period.

Suggested KPIs: - Procurement Spend - Average Purchase Order Value -
Unit Cost vs Target Price - Estimated Defect Cost Exposure - Potential
Unit Margin %, if reliable cost and price data is available

7.  MATERIALS AND SUPPLY CHAIN RISK

Recommendations: - Identify materials that are both costly and used by
many products. - Assess dependency on a single supplier for critical
materials. - Review standard cost, supplier rating, lead time, and
availability together rather than relying on cost alone. - Maintain
approved alternatives for critical materials where technically and
commercially feasible. - Validate the product-material bridge table and
dependency calculations before publishing risk rankings.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Materials - Total Products - Product-Material
Dependencies - Material Dependency Count - Material Standard Cost -
High-Cost Materials - Supplier Dependence / Risk Level, if available

8.  OPERATOR AND SHIFT PERFORMANCE

Recommendations: - Compare operators using production volume, defect
rate, and downtime together, with suitable context for product
difficulty and hours worked. - Compare shifts using production, quality,
downtime, and energy indicators. - Use results to identify training
needs and process improvements, not to make decisions from a single
metric alone. - Confirm each operator ID maps to the correct production
and downtime records. - If all operators show identical values, validate
active relationships and test measures in a table visual before
interpreting charts.

Suggested KPIs: - Total Operators - Production by Operator / Shift -
Defect Rate by Operator / Shift - Downtime by Operator / Shift - Average
Experience Years

9.  POWER BI REPORT DESIGN AND GOVERNANCE

Recommendations: - Keep definitions consistent across all nine report
pages. - Use a shared Date table and shared Plant and Product dimensions
where appropriate. - Confirm slicers filter every relevant visual and
test cross-filter interactions. - Show units on KPI cards (units,
minutes, kWh, currency) and format percentages correctly. - Add tooltips
for definitions and a note describing the selected date range. -
Validate headline totals against source tables and document refresh
date, data owner, and measure definitions. - Avoid interpreting 100%
stacked charts as actual production totals; use regular stacked or
clustered charts when actual quantities are required.

10. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

Immediate (Week 1–2): - Validate data quality, table relationships, KPI
formulas, and slicer behavior. - Identify the top verified production
shortfalls, defect contributors, downtime causes, and supplier delays.

Short term (Month 1): - Assign owners to the highest-priority issues. -
Start root-cause analysis for quality and downtime. - Review supplier
performance and high-cost materials.

Medium term (Months 2–3): - Evaluate improvement results against the
baseline. - Refine preventive maintenance and energy-efficiency
actions. - Review material alternatives and supplier-continuity plans.

Ongoing: - Refresh the dashboard on an agreed schedule. - Review KPI
trends with responsible teams. - Record action owners, due dates,
status, and measured impact.

SUCCESS MEASUREMENT

For each recommendation, record: - Baseline value and baseline period -
Target value and target date - Responsible owner - Planned action -
Status - Actual measured result - Data source and validation notes

FINAL NOTE

These are recommendations for analysis and planning, not proof that a
specific plant, supplier, machine, or operator is underperforming.
Validate the data, consider operational context, and involve responsible
teams before making business decisions.
