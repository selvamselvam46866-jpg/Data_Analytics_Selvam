AEROHOME MANUFACTURING — KEY INSIGHTS

Purpose

This file summarizes the key business insights for the AeroHome
Manufacturing Power BI dashboard. The figures below are based on the
dashboard values shared in the project conversation and should be
validated against the final filtered report before presenting them as
official results.

1.  EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

-   Total planned units: approximately 1,500,000.
-   Total produced units: approximately 1,325,480.
-   Production achievement: approximately 88.4% in the original
    Executive Overview dashboard example.
-   Total defective units: approximately 72,560.
-   Defect rate: approximately 5.5%.
-   Total downtime: approximately 48,320 minutes.
-   Total energy consumed: approximately 1,254,600 kWh.
-   Procurement spend: approximately $2,845,600.
-   Estimated defect cost exposure: approximately $362,170.

Insight: Production is below the planned volume in the example
dashboard, while defects and downtime are important areas for
operational review. Confirm that all KPIs use the same date range and
slicer selections before comparing them.

2.  PRODUCTION AND PLANT PERFORMANCE

-   Compare planned units against produced units by month and plant.
-   Identify plants with the largest production shortfall, rather than
    relying only on total production.
-   Review monthly production achievement to identify periods of
    improvement or decline.
-   Compare production across product categories to understand which
    categories contribute most to output.

Recommended actions: - Review shortfall by plant, product, and shift. -
Investigate months with lower production achievement. - Use the date,
plant, and product-category slicers to isolate patterns.

3.  MACHINE AND DOWNTIME PERFORMANCE

-   Total downtime in the example dashboard is 48,320 minutes.
-   Review downtime by machine, operator, plant, and reason where those
    fields exist.
-   Rank the largest downtime contributors to help prioritize
    investigation.

Recommended actions: - Focus maintenance reviews on the machines or
causes with the highest recorded downtime. - Check whether downtime is
concentrated in particular shifts or plants. - Verify operator and
machine relationships in the data model if every category displays the
same downtime total.

4.  QUALITY ANALYSIS

-   The example dashboard shows 72,560 defective units and a 5.5% defect
    rate.
-   Compare defect rates by product, plant, machine, and operator.
-   Review estimated defect cost exposure alongside defect counts to
    prioritize financially significant quality issues.

Recommended actions: - Investigate products with high defect rates and
high defect cost exposure. - Validate the defect-rate calculation as
total defective units divided by total produced units. - Confirm that
the operator, plant, and product filters correctly affect both numerator
and denominator.

5.  OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY AND ENERGY

-   The example dashboard shows energy consumption of 1,254,600 kWh.
-   Track energy per unit as total energy consumed divided by total
    produced units.
-   Compare energy consumption and production achievement across plants.
-   Review shift-level production and defect rates to identify
    operational differences.

Recommended actions: - Investigate high energy per unit after accounting
for product mix and production volume. - Compare similar plants and
shifts before concluding that one is less efficient. - Ensure energy and
production facts are connected to shared date and plant dimensions where
appropriate.

6.  FINANCE AND COST ANALYSIS

-   Example procurement spend: $2,845,600.
-   Example estimated defect cost exposure: $362,170.
-   Compare material standard cost, purchase spend, and defect cost
    exposure.
-   Review unit cost versus target price by product if those fields are
    available.

Recommended actions: - Prioritize high-spend materials for purchasing
review. - Investigate high defect-cost products and their underlying
quality patterns. - Confirm currency and cost definitions before making
financial comparisons.

7.  PROCUREMENT AND SUPPLIER PERFORMANCE

-   Example dashboard values include 1,250 purchase orders, 210 delayed
    orders, and a 16.8% delay rate.
-   Compare expected and actual lead times by supplier.
-   Review supplier spend alongside delivery status and supplier
    ratings.

Recommended actions: - Review suppliers with repeated delays or longer
actual lead times. - Consider spend, delay rate, and supplier rating
together rather than using one metric alone. - Validate that
purchase-order counts use distinct purchase-order IDs.

8.  MATERIALS AND SUPPLY CHAIN RISK

-   The example dashboard shows 58 materials, 24 products, and 186
    product-material dependencies.
-   Compare material dependency counts with standard cost.
-   Review high-cost materials and materials used across many products.
-   Compare procurement spend by material and supplier dependence where
    data is available.

Recommended actions: - Investigate materials that are both expensive and
used by many products. - Validate the material-to-product bridge table
and its relationships before interpreting dependency counts. - Treat a
high cost or high dependency as a potential exposure indicator, not
proof of an actual supply disruption.

9.  OPERATOR AND SHIFT PERFORMANCE

-   Compare production, defective units, defect rate, downtime, and
    experience by operator.
-   Compare production volume, defects, downtime, and energy across Day,
    Evening, and Night shifts.
-   Use operator-level rankings to identify where additional
    investigation may be useful.

Recommended actions: - Validate that each operator ID maps to the
correct production and downtime records. - If every operator shows the
same downtime or defect rate, check active one-to-many relationships and
filter context. - Avoid drawing conclusions from charts until
operator-level totals have been tested in a table visual.

10. DATA QUALITY AND REPORT VALIDATION

Before presenting the dashboard: - Confirm all report pages use the
intended date range. - Test date, plant, product category, material, and
shift slicers. - Reconcile KPI totals with the underlying fact tables. -
Check that dimension keys are unique and relationships are active. -
Confirm percentages are formatted as percentages, not raw decimals. -
Confirm currency units and display units (K/M/B) are consistent. - Avoid
mixing figures from different dashboard versions or slicer states.

FINAL SUMMARY

The AeroHome dashboard is intended to help managers monitor production
attainment, quality, downtime, energy, cost, procurement, material
dependency, and workforce performance. The strongest next step is to
investigate the largest verified shortfalls and cost exposures, then
validate causes at plant, product, machine, supplier, and shift level.

IMPORTANT NOTE

The values in this file are example figures from the project dashboard
conversation, not independently verified live business results. Some
screenshots show different values because of different pages or
dashboard versions. Use the final Power BI report’s current filter
context as the source of truth.
